**Change:** foam.
- Per-cascade Jacobian injection with decay, prewarmed over 12 s for stills.
- Rendered through the Blender-baked texture as a dissolving threshold field: dense whitewater when fresh; with age it opens holes at the lace cell centres, then leaves thin nets and wind streaks.

**Value structure:**
- **`dusk__sea_high`:** now has true highlights: whitecap patches reach the top of the range (p95 0.71).
- **`dusk__sea_low`:** the near wave face carries a pale net of aged foam over dark water. The reference's foreground has the same marbled read.
- **Night:** remains near black (mean 0.016). Foam is present but invisible without exposure.

**Color:** unchanged from 0005. The copper sky still tints everything that reflects it.

**Foam and spray read:**
- **Better:** from 25 m the sea finally reads as a gale. Discrete breaking patches trail each crest, with streaks downwind of them, and the patch shapes are organic, not tiled.
- **Worse:**
  - Up close, the net on the near face is too uniform in cell size, like cracked glaze.
  - A few long wind streaks run straight at the camera and read as scratches.
  - There is no spray yet above the breaking crest, which is where the reference is brightest.

**Sky:** placeholder.

**Better than the reference:** foam is simulated, not painted. Patches form where crests actually break, and decay as the wave moves on, so the pattern is coherent with the wave field in every shot.

**Worse than the reference:**
- Whitecap coverage is still lower than a Beaufort 10 sea.
- The near-face net lacks the reference's variety of cell sizes.
- No spindrift.

**Regressions:** none; changes are intended.

**Next fix:** the storm sky (step 6), which changes every reflection and sets the scene's value range. Then spindrift (step 8), for the backlit spray above the crest.
