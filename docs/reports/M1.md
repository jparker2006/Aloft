# Milestone 1 report: hero still scene

Branch `claude/keen-euler-s4hkre`. Everything here was built and reviewed in a cloud container with no GPU:
captures ran on headless Chromium 141 with WebGPU on SwiftShader (a CPU rasterizer). **No number in
this report is a performance measurement.** Frame times must come from the target machine; see "Run
and measure on the Mac" below.

## Summary

Milestone 1 is complete: the hero still scene renders at every bookmark and both presets, mid-flash included, with no console errors. Every system in the milestone is in place except SSR and GTAO, which have nothing to act on until the ship exists (see known issues). What was built:

- **Ocean:**
  - JONSWAP plus swell with resolution-independent seeding.
  - Three FFT cascades in compute (1024, 173 and 31 m patches).
  - Choppy displacement with Jacobian.
  - A snapped geometry clipmap with morphing and a horizon skirt.
- **Foam:**
  - Per-cascade Jacobian injection with decay, prewarmed for stills.
  - Rendered through a Blender-baked texture (`tools/blender/foam_texture.py`) as a dissolving threshold field, stretched downwind.
- **Sea shading:** Fresnel reflection of the clouds, a clamped GGX sun glint, crest subsurface glow, rain ripples and flash lighting.
- **Sky:**
  - A half-resolution volumetric storm deck with temporal accumulation.
  - A Worley underside of rolls and pouches.
  - Diffuse light through the deck, far-field sun occlusion, warm underlight toward the dusk gap, and self-shadowed scud.
- **Lightning:**
  - Seeded strikes with bolt ribbons, lighting the deck from inside, the sea, the haze and the rain curtains.
  - A limiter of three full-screen flashes per second (unit tested).
  - "Reduce flashing", `?flash=` freeze frames, and a first-run notice that holds lightning back until answered.
- **Particles:** compute rain in a camera-local volume, and spindrift torn from breaking crests as wind-stretched wisps.
- **Atmosphere:** analytic height fog whose sun lobe is confined to the gap, plus a half-res raymarch of lit rain curtains.
- **Post:**
  - TRAA, motion blur, bloom, flash-excluded auto exposure and lens rain.
  - ACES-fitted tone mapping, chosen by capture.
  - A grade measured from the references, vignette, grain and dither.
- **Performance:** tiers from a startup benchmark, a seven-rung degradation ladder with hysteresis (the ocean last), `compileAsync` warmup, per-pass A/B flags and the F3 profiler.
- **Tooling:** shot mode, the capture harness (contact sheets, SSIM baselines, snapshot captures), GPU correctness tests for the FFT and the noise volumes, 53 unit tests, and a free camera.

### Better than the references

- **Coherence.** Every image comes from one world: the same sea, deck and light seen from eight views. The references are separate generated images whose sun, clouds and sea disagree from frame to frame.
- **Lightning.** A strike lights the deck from inside and reveals its pouches and rolls, lit on the side facing the channel and dark behind. It lays a reflection column on the sea and lights the rain around it. The night flash frames are close to the references' brightness (`sea_low` 0.28 against 0.21) and show cloud structure a single photograph cannot.
- **The dusk sun path.** A gold glitter column on dark water under a torn shelf, with emerald subsurface in the backlit crests.
- **Foam that belongs to the waves.** Whitewater appears where crests break, ages into lace downwind, and dissolves; the references' foam is painted per image.
- **Safety.** The flash rate is limited and tested, "Reduce flashing" cuts a flash frame's brightness by more than half (0.40 to 0.17 in `0015-reduceflash`), and no flash reaches the screen before the photosensitivity notice.

### Worse than the references

- **Breaking waves.** An FFT sea cannot overturn: no plunging lips, no curling crests. The references' most dramatic element (the backlit breaking crest in `sea_low`) is beyond this technique; milestone 4 or 5 would need crest particles or a breaking-wave mesh.
- **Whitewater and spray volume.** Our Beaufort 10 sea carries less whitewater and far less spray than the references.
- **Cloud detail.** The references' clouds are sculpted and crisp. Ours are softer, limited by the half-resolution march, the detail noise resolution and the step budget.
- **The dusk gap.** It lacks the saturated cloud edges and the dark rain shafts that make the references' sunsets. The rain curtains exist but read faintly.
- **`horizon_lightning`'s flash** is twice as bright as its reference: a closer, taller bolt whose lit deck fills the upper frame.

2. **Look at it in motion.** Stills cannot show TAA ghosting, shimmer at the horizon or in foam, rain and spray in motion, or motion blur. Fly around with the free camera and tell me what bothers you.

## Run and measure on the Mac

1. Install and start:
   ```
   git clone https://github.com/jparker2006/Aloft && cd Aloft
   git checkout claude/keen-euler-s4hkre
   npm ci
   npm run dev
   ```
2. Open the printed URL in **current Chrome, or Safari 26+** (WebGPU). The first run shows the photosensitivity notice. Lightning stays dark until it is answered.
3. **Controls (free camera, not gameplay):**
   - Click to capture the mouse, move with WASD, Q/E down and up, Shift for speed, Esc to release.
   - 1 to 4 jump to the bookmarks (`sea_low`, `sea_high`, `horizon_lightning`, `storm_sky`).
   - P blends between dusk and night (2.5 s).
   - L forces a lightning strike.
4. **Measure:**
   - Press **F3** (or open with `?prof`) for the profiler: frame time, GPU time from timestamp queries (Chrome; Safari falls back to frame time), CPU time per system, draw calls, internal resolution, tier and ladder rung.
   - The tier is chosen by a 2 s startup benchmark. Force one with `?q=low|med|high`; override a single field with `?qset=cloudDeckSteps:20`.
   - For budget numbers, run at the default tier with the window at its normal size, let it settle for 10 s at each bookmark, and read GPU ms. The ladder may step down on its own; the overlay shows the rung.
5. **A/B flags** for per-pass cost:
   - `?nolightning` `?norain` `?nospray` `?nofog` `?novolume`
   - `?notaa` `?nomotionblur` `?nobloom` `?nolensrain` `?noexposure` `?nograde`
   - `?tonemap=agx` (ACES-fitted is the default), `?reduceflash`
6. **Captures on the Mac** (real GPU, much faster):
   ```
   npx playwright install chromium
   ALOFT_HW_GPU=1 npm run capture
   ```
   `ALOFT_HW_GPU=1` launches Chromium with its normal GPU path instead of SwiftShader. The contact sheet lands in `captures/<run>/sheet.jpg`.

## Known issues

- **Performance is unmeasured.** The cloud march is the largest cost and the biggest risk: up to 28 deck and 8 scud steps at half resolution, with four light probes per step. If the Mac cannot hold 60 fps, the first ladder rung cuts the steps by a third.
- **Spectrum rebuilds run on the main thread** (158 ms for a full rebuild). They only happen when the weather changes past a threshold, never during a still; a worker is planned for milestone 5, when weather escalates during play.
- **The last ladder rung time-slices the fine ocean cascade** (updates it every other frame) rather than dropping its resolution from 256 to 128 as SPEC section 3 describes. Changing resolution would recompile the FFT pipelines, which the ladder must never do.
- **SSR and GTAO are not in the milestone 1 post graph.** The sea reflects the sky and clouds analytically. There is no geometry for screen-space reflections or ambient occlusion to act on until the ship arrives (milestone 4).
- **Two workarounds for the capture browser**, both capture-only (`tools/capture/compat.mjs`):
  - It rejects three.js's identity texture swizzle.
  - It cannot lazy-clear 3D textures with render attachment usage.
  Neither ships in the game. If the Mac console shows a WebGPU validation error mentioning a 3D texture view, that second issue exists there too; please report it.
- **The profiler's draw-call and triangle counts undercount** (they show only a few draws). Frame time and GPU time, the numbers that matter for the budget, are unaffected.
- **"No shimmer at the horizon or in foam at 1080p"** can only be judged in motion on real hardware. The stills show no tiling. Foam octaves fade to their means with distance, and the clipmap morphs between levels.
